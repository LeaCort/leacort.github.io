Privacy policy

Version 1, 13 September 2026.

Secret Rules is a party game played out loud, around a single phone. This page explains what the app keeps on the device, what it does not collect, and what the advertising it shows does.

Who publishes the app

Léa Cortembert, independent publisher, Switzerland. Contact: secretrulesgame@gmail.com.

No account, no sign-up

The app asks for no account, no email address and no phone number. Playing works entirely offline.

What stays on the device

The app stores on the phone, and only there:

  • the first names entered for a game, so the same group can be offered next time;
  • the list of rules already played, so they are not dealt again too soon;
  • your settings: language, sounds, vibrations;
  • the game in progress, so it can be resumed if the app is closed;
  • the age range recorded by the start screen (under 13, 13 to 15, 16 and over, or not given). The year of birth itself is not kept.

None of this is sent to the publisher or to anyone else. Uninstalling the app erases all of it. The settings also let you reset the list of played rules and correct the age range.

Advertising

The app shows full-screen ads between some rounds, provided by Google AdMob. For this, Google's advertising module may receive technical information about the device, such as the IP address or technical identifiers, under Google's privacy policy.

The app is designed for an audience that includes children:

  • every ad is requested with the "child" or "minor" treatment that matches the recorded age range, and with a general-audience content rating;
  • no personalised advertising is requested for anyone under 16 or whose age range is not given;
  • a person aged 16 or over may, in the European Economic Area and the United Kingdom, be asked to consent to personalised advertising, and can change that choice at any time from the settings;
  • the app never asks for tracking permission and does not use the device's advertising identifier for tracking purposes.

No ad is shown during a round, nor when the app starts.

Protecting children

A neutral screen asks for the year of birth at first launch, without saying what it changes. Anything to do with money, data or a text like this one sits behind an arithmetic question that a young child cannot solve.

Your rights

As the publisher holds no data about you, there is nothing to ask her to access, correct or erase: everything is on your device, under your control. For the technical data processed by Google for advertising, requests go to Google.

For any question, write to the contact address given above.

Applicable law

This policy is governed by Swiss law, in particular the Federal Act on Data Protection. People living in the European Union also have the rights provided by the General Data Protection Regulation.

Changes

Any change to this policy is published in a new version of the app, with its date. The version in force is the one shown here.
